Why MI fits social work

Social workers often meet people who did not choose the conversation: mandated clients, parents in child protection, people in crisis. Confrontation tends to produce defensiveness. MI is a guiding style that helps people explore ambivalence and find their own reasons to change, while respecting autonomy.

The spirit of MI

Partnership
Work alongside the person as an expert on their own life, rather than act as the expert on them.
Acceptance
Respect their worth, autonomy and strengths; accurate empathy; affirm. Accepting a person is not approving of every behaviour.
Compassion
Actively promote the person's welfare and give priority to their needs.
Evocation
Draw out the person's own motivations and ideas rather than installing them.

The four processes

  1. Engaging: build a working relationship.
  2. Focusing: agree a direction for the conversation.
  3. Evoking: draw out reasons and confidence for change.
  4. Planning: develop commitment and a concrete plan, if and when the person is ready.

OARS: the core skills

SkillWhat it doesExample
Open questionsInvite the person to elaborate"What would you like to be different?"
AffirmationsRecognise strengths and effort"It took courage to come in today."
ReflectionsShow understanding; deepen exploration"You love your kids and drinking is getting in the way of that."
SummariesCollect and link what has been said"So on one hand... and on the other... Where does that leave you?"

Aim for more reflections than questions. Asking many questions in a row can feel like an interrogation.

Change talk and sustain talk

Change talk (favours change)Sustain talk (favours the status quo)
"I want to cut down." "I could try." "I need to for the kids.""I don't have a problem." "I can't cope without it." "It's not that bad."

Notice and reflect change talk, and ask for elaboration ("Say more about that"). Respond to sustain talk without arguing: a reflection, an amplified or double-sided reflection, or an emphasis on personal choice. Discord, such as arguing or defensiveness, is a signal to change approach, not to push harder.

Practice: choose a response

A client says: "Everyone keeps telling me I drink too much. I don't see what the big deal is." Which response is most consistent with MI?

MI in your setting

Child protection and statutory work

Be transparent about your role and what must happen regardless. Within those limits, explore what the parent wants for their children and family. Autonomy exists inside the non-negotiables.

Hospital and emergency

Brief contacts: a short, respectful conversation, one or two reflections, an offer of follow-up and a warm referral. An admission can be a teachable moment but not a time for lengthy persuasion.

Community and home visits

Build trust over repeated contacts. Use outreach flexibly and follow the person's agenda: housing, money and relationships often come first.
